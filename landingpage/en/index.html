FIELD RADIATION SURVEYS · GPS · REPORTING

Every reading located and documented, from the field to the finished report.

RadRecon turns your smartphone into a complete field tool for gamma dose rate surveys. Each measurement point is captured with GPS position, timestamp, instrument and photo in a single step, so the paper log sheet and the spreadsheet re-entry that follows it are no longer needed.

A nonprofit civic initiative

RadRecon is built to make the administration around work with radiation exposure accurate, quick and efficient, so that professionals can spend their time on radiation protection instead of paperwork.

Free for everyone

RadRecon is free to use: no fees, no subscription, no account.

Public source code

The source code is public on GitHub under the Apache License 2.0: free to use, modify and redistribute, including commercially, as long as the copyright and license notices are kept. We are looking for volunteers to test and develop it.

Less paperwork, more accuracy

Administrative procedures tied to work with radiation exposure should be sufficiently accurate, take as little time as possible and be as efficient as possible. That is the only goal of this initiative.

Everything a survey needs, in one app

Replace log sheets, spreadsheets and loose photo folders with one structured record that is created on site.

GPS-tied point logging

Every reading is stamped with position, accuracy radius, time, instrument, value and unit, plus an optional label, note and photo.

Survey map

All points of a survey on one map, so coverage gaps and hot spots are visible while you are still on site. Save a map image to attach to the report.

One-tap protocol export

Export a Word protocol with the point list, map and photos, or hand the data on as JSON or CSV for further processing.

Built around the way surveys are actually done

From the first reading to the final report, in three steps.

1

Start a survey

Give it a name and a location, and assign the personnel who take part in the measurement.

2

Record points

Choose the measurement type, enter the value or read it from a connected instrument, and capture the GPS position and a photo. Alert thresholds warn you immediately.

3

Review and report

Check the map, close the survey and export the protocol. Data can move to another RadRecon installation as JSON.

More than a log sheet

The tools you would otherwise carry in a separate notebook, on the same device you record with.

Live distance and bearing

Walk back to a logged point, or keep a fixed distance and heading from a source, with a continuously updated arrow and range readout.

Isotope calculator

Convert activity to dose rate and back for Co-60, Cs-137, Ir-192, Ra-226, I-131, Na-22 and Am-241, or enter a custom nuclide.

Source search

A guided approach to a source, based on the gradient of the readings you have recorded so far.

Instruments and Modbus/TCP

Keep a library of instruments with calibration date and factor, and read values from WiFi Modbus/TCP meters straight into the entry form.

Automatic logging

Record points from a Modbus-connected instrument at a time or distance interval while you walk.

Source and personnel registry

Track sources with decay-corrected activity and compare calculated with measured dose rate. Optional reminders for service life, inspection, medical exam and training expiry.

Dose records and multiple employers

Personal doses and company data are managed in the same app, kept apart where they must be kept apart. The dose figures in RadRecon support your record keeping and do not replace official personal dosimetry.

Dose collection

Collect doses per person during a survey: integrated automatically from a connected Modbus detector during automatic logging, or entered manually, and still adjustable when the survey is closed.

Annual dose per person

Add passive dosimetry results (monthly or bimonthly evaluation) and see each person’s annual dose, split into online, manual and passive. Doses can be imported and exported as CSV.

Switching between companies

If you work for more than one company, keep each company’s data separate and switch with a tap. The current state is saved automatically when you switch, and a company can be moved to a new phone from a backup file.

RadRecon in the field

A clear interface designed for outdoor use on a phone, in light or dark theme.

One record from the first reading to the final report

Paper and spreadsheets lose information between the field and the report. RadRecon closes that gap.

No re-typing

Frequently asked questions

Does RadRecon need an internet connection?

No. Recording, calculating and exporting work offline. The map background is loaded from OpenStreetMap, so the map tiles appear only while a connection is available.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no registration and no cloud. Your data is stored on your device and leaves it only when you export or share it.

Which instruments can I use?

Any instrument: type the reading in manually. Instruments that are reachable over WiFi Modbus/TCP can also be read directly into the entry form.

Can I rely on the isotope calculator for official records?

The gamma constants and half-lives are reference values. Verify them against an authoritative source before you document a result in an official protocol.

Which platforms and languages are supported?

Android, iPhone, Windows, macOS and Linux. The app is available in English and Hungarian, and follows the light, dark or system theme.

Does automatic logging run in the background?

No. Logging runs only while its screen is open. If the phone locks or you switch apps, it pauses and has to be restarted.

How do I get my data out?

Export a Word protocol with photos, the points as CSV for Excel, or as JSON that another RadRecon installation can import.

Is RadRecon really free?

Yes. It is a nonprofit civic initiative: free to use, with no account and no subscription. The source code is public on GitHub under the Apache License 2.0 (free to use, modify and redistribute, including commercially, with the notices kept).

Does RadRecon replace official dosimetry?

No. The doses collected in RadRecon (integrated from a detector, entered manually or taken from passive dosimetry results) support your record keeping. Official personal doses come from your approved dosimetry service, so check the figures against it.

Can I work for more than one company?

Yes. Each company has its own data, and the company switcher moves between them with a tap. The current state is saved automatically when you switch.
